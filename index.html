Around

Drives and detours.

Inside ninety minutes you have a UNESCO mountain palace complex, the westernmost point of continental Europe, two fishing ports, medieval walled towns, the silver-coast brandy region, and Portugal's capital. The eight days are not enough. Pick three.

The N247 coast — Peniche to Ericeira

The single best drive in the region. Cliffs, fishing villages, surf beaches, vineyards. Stops at Vimeiro, Areia Branca, Santa Cruz, Praia Azul.

2.5h with stops · paved · low traffic

Serra de Sintra loop

N375 climbs into the Sintra mountains — hairpins, eucalyptus shade. Drop down to Cabo da Roca, then the coast road into Cascais.

3h with stops · paved · busy at weekends

Inland wine country

Bombarral → Óbidos → Alcobaça. Vineyards, the walled medieval town, the cistercian monastery and the tomb of Pedro and Inês.

Half day · paved · cultural-heavy

Nazaré via São Martinho

Shell-shaped sheltered bay at São Martinho do Porto, then up to Nazaré and the funicular to Sítio for the giant-wave canyon viewpoint.

Full day · paved · dramatic

Mafra & the royal road

The Palácio Nacional de Mafra is the largest baroque palace in Portugal; the royal hunting forest is open to visitors. Quiet inland alternative to the motorway.

Half day · paved · low-key

Sintra town

UNESCO, four fairy-tale palaces in walking distance, tourist-heavy by 10am. Go early or late. Monserrate is the quietest and arguably the best.

Full day · steep · busy

Things worth doing

The week's menu.

No fixed plan. These are the things worth one of your eight days — pick, combine, vote in the chat.

  • Skate

    Bowl session at Peniche

    Concrete bowl on the headland, ocean on three sides. The trip's anchor day.

  • Skate

    Long cruise: Estoril → Cascais

    Seven uninterrupted kilometres of smooth tile along the Atlantic. The longest roll on the menu.

  • Skate

    Lisbon urban route

    LX Factory, riverside to MAAT, Praça do Comércio. Plan it, eat well, come home tired.

  • See

    Museu da Lourinhã — dinosaurs

    One of the only nests of dinosaur eggs with embryos ever found. Tue–Sun, closed Mondays. An hour, well spent.

  • See

    Cabo da Roca

    The westernmost point of continental Europe. A lighthouse, a wall, a cliff, a wind. Worth the drive for an hour.

  • See

    Walled Óbidos

    Medieval town, cobbled, hostile to wheels, gorgeous. Drink a ginjinha from a chocolate cup.

  • Eat

    Saturday market in Caldas da Rainha

    One of Portugal's best. Pair with the Caldas skatepark and the Bordallo Pinheiro ceramics factory.

  • Eat

    Aguardente tasting

    Lourinhã is one of only three demarcated brandy regions in Europe — the other two are Cognac and Armagnac. The cooperative does tastings; book ahead via Visit Lourinhã.

  • Rest

    A beach day at Areia Branca

    Cliff-backed Atlantic beach four kilometres west. Body in the water, lunch on the seafront, no board for a day.

Eat, shop, drink

Food, supplies and where the night goes.

Supermarkets

  • Pingo Doce — Portuguese chain, the everyday shop. Lourinhã town has at least one.
  • Lidl / Intermarché — on the edge of town, cheaper for bulk.
  • Continente, Caldas da Rainha — the big weekly-shop hypermarket if the house is self-catering. Worth a single drive on day one.

Restaurant categories (specific names go in the chat)

  • Marisqueira — shellfish house. Order arroz de marisco for the table.
  • Casa de pasto — neighbourhood cantine, fixed lunch menu €10–15.
  • Tasca — wine-led small joint, family-run, petiscos.
  • Churrasqueira — chicken on a grill, group-friendly, cheap.

Order, at least once each: caldeirada, bacalhau à brás, polvo à lagareiro, sardinhas assadas.

Nightlife

  • Praia da Areia Branca — seafront bars, mellow.
  • Peniche — surf-bar culture, especially around the Baleal causeway.
  • Ericeira — the busiest scene within easy reach.
  • Caldas da Rainha — student town energy, quieter than the coast.
  • Lisbon — Bairro Alto and Cais do Sodré — a proper big-city night. Designated driver or hotel.

Get yourself out of trouble

Portuguese, for skating and for the rest.

Portuguese isn't Spanish. The vowels swallow themselves and locals will be patient with anyone who tries.

The unavoidables

OláHello
Bom dia / Boa tarde / Boa noiteGood morning / afternoon / evening
Obrigado / ObrigadaThank you — ending matches who says it, not who you're thanking
Por favorPlease
DesculpeSorry / excuse me
Fala inglês?Do you speak English?
A conta, por favorThe bill, please
Duas cervejas, por favorTwo beers, please
Saúde!Cheers

Actually useful for skating

Onde fica a pista de skate?Where's the skatepark?
Podemos andar de skate aqui?Can we skate here?
Sabe um sítio bom para andar?Do you know a good spot to skate?
O chão é liso?Is the surface smooth?
Onde compro rodas / lixa?Where can I buy wheels / griptape?
Foi brutal!That was sick! (Locals use it too.)
Estou bem.I'm fine. (Says the person on the floor.)

Phrases that buy time when something has gone wrong

Desculpe, foi sem querer.Sorry, it wasn't on purpose.
Desculpe a parede, foi o vento.Sorry about the wall, it was the wind.
Pago já.I'll pay right now. (Magic words.)
Já vamos embora, prometo.We're leaving right now, I promise.
Não sou eu o líder, é aquele.I'm not the leader, he is. (Point at the most innocent member of the group.)
Precisamos de gelo. Para o joelho, não para a bebida.We need ice. For the knee, not the drink.
Já morreu alguém neste corrimão?Has anyone died on this rail?
O segurança é rápido?Is the security guard fast?
Somos vinte e um. Faltam dois.There are twenty-one of us. Two are missing.
O meu amigo diz que está bem, mas não está.My friend says he's fine, but he's not.
Mais duas, e uma para o motorista que não bebe.Two more, and one for the driver who isn't drinking.
Para onde vai a noite depois da meia-noite?Where does the night go after midnight?

Portuguese eats final vowels and slurs unstressed ones. Obrigado sounds closer to brigadu, está sounds like shtah. Lean into it.

The boring but important

Practical.

Getting there

Fly Lisbon (LIS). A8 motorway north, around an hour. Rent a car between two or three of you — public transport burns the day. Toll transponder via the rental desk.

Weather

Late May / early June: mid-20s °C, mostly dry, reliably windy after lunch. Layer up for the evening — the Atlantic cools things off quickly.

Money & plugs

Euros. Cards work everywhere. €30 in cash for markets and small bars. Plugs Type F.

Emergencies

112 — all services. English is understood. Hospital is in Caldas da Rainha; health centre in Lourinhã.

Pads

Bring them. Concrete bowls and Atlantic salt damp are unforgiving.

Group comms

A WhatsApp / Signal group will be circulating before arrival. Post your number in the chat if you're not in it yet.
