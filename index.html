The town

Lourinhã is a small town on Portugal's Oeste coast, about 65 km north of Lisbon. Population sits around 25,000 across the wider municipality, but the centre itself is walkable in twenty minutes end to end. Whitewashed houses, a couple of decent squares, a Manueline‑era church, and — three kilometres west — a string of cliff‑backed Atlantic beaches.

Two things the town is genuinely known for:

  • Dinosaurs. The cliffs around Praia da Areia Branca and Porto Dinheiro have produced some of Europe's most important Late Jurassic fossils. The Museu da Lourinhã in the centre is small but worth an hour — they have one of the only nests of dinosaur eggs with embryos inside ever found.
  • Aguardente. The DOC Aguardente Vínica Lourinhã is one of only three demarcated brandy regions in Europe. The other two are Cognac and Armagnac. Not a bad evening drink after a long session.

The wind is the thing to know about. Lourinhã is exposed to the nortada, the northerly that picks up most afternoons in late spring. Skate the bowls in the morning, hide in the lee of buildings or head to a sheltered street spot after lunch.

Where to skate

A note on accuracy: skateparks come and go, and surfaces re‑pour. Every spot below is worth a check on a satellite map and a recent Instagram search before committing a day to it. Local shops will give you the best read on what's actually rolling that week.

Around Lourinhã itself

The town centre doesn't have a marquee skatepark, but the seafront promenade at Praia da Areia Branca (4 km west, regular buses) has long stretches of smooth tile and a few ledges and banks worth a cruise. Mornings before the wind comes up.

For street, the square in front of the Câmara Municipal and the paved area by the Mercado Municipal are the obvious starting points — granite ledges, polished stone, generally tolerant if you turn up before the shops open or after they close.

Peniche — 25 km north

Peniche is the surf town of central Portugal, and its Skatepark da Papoa sits on the headland looking out at the lighthouse. Concrete bowl plus a street section, ocean on three sides. Forty minutes by car, longer by the Rodoviária do Tejo bus. The single most rewarding day trip on the list.

Caldas da Rainha — 30 km north

Bigger inland town, decent municipal park, and a much livelier scene than anywhere on the coast. Worth pairing with the Saturday market (one of the best in Portugal) and a stop at the Bordallo Pinheiro ceramics factory if your skating partner has any interest in not‑skating.

Torres Vedras — 30 km south

Closer to Lisbon, bigger town, and the skatepark gets recommended by people who actually live in the region. Easy detour on the day Jim arrives or leaves if he's coming through Lisbon.

Ericeira — 40 km south

A World Surfing Reserve, so the vibe is unmistakable, and there's a small park plus a lot of smooth promenade. Touristy in a way Lourinhã isn't, but the food makes up for it.

Lisbon, if a full day opens up

An hour south on the A8. LX Factory, the MAAT riverside, and the marble plazas of Praça do Comércio and Terreiro do Paço are the obvious cruising routes. The DIY scene in Marvila is worth asking a local about.

Local intel: Ondamigas (Praia da Areia Branca) and the surf shops along the Peniche seafront tend to know who's skating where. A coffee and a question goes further than any guidebook.

Things that tend to land in this window

None of these are guaranteed for 2026 — annual programmes shift — but they are the kind of thing to look for:

  • Festas do Concelho da Lourinhã — the town's municipal festival, historically clustered around early summer. Concerts in the main square, food stalls, bullring events, fireworks one night.
  • Dia da Cidade / Feriado Municipal — towns in the Oeste celebrate their municipal day with a public holiday and a programme of cultural events. Worth checking whether Lourinhã's falls this week.
  • Surf contests at Peniche / Supertubos — late spring is a quieter contest window than autumn, but local club competitions run through the season.
  • Wine and aguardente tastings at the cooperative just outside town — bookable through Visit Lourinhã.
  • Markets — Lourinhã runs a monthly fair (Feira Mensal); the date moves, so check the Câmara's calendar for whether one falls in the trip window. Caldas da Rainha's Saturday market runs every week without fail — 30 May and 6 June are both in scope.

Confirmed weekly fixtures

  • Caldas da Rainha market — every Saturday morning. Catch it on the way in (30 May) or out (6 June).
  • Museu da Lourinhã — open Tue–Sun, closed Mondays. Plan a rainy afternoon around it.

Practical bits

Getting there

Fly Lisbon (LIS). A8 motorway north, roughly an hour to Lourinhã. Renting a car is the right move — buses exist but eat the day. Toll roads use an electronic transponder; the rental desk will sort it.

Weather, late May / early June

Mid‑20s °C, occasional cloud, mostly dry, reliably windy after lunch. Pack a sweatshirt for the evening — the Atlantic cools things off quickly once the sun drops.

Language

Portuguese. English is fine in surf and skate shops and at most restaurants in Areia Branca. Inland and in smaller cafés, a handful of words goes a long way: obrigado, bom dia, uma bica se faz favor.

Money & plugs

Euros. Cards work almost everywhere, but keep €30 in cash for markets and small cafés. Plugs are Type F (the European two‑round‑pin).

Eat

Grilled fish at any marisqueira on the seafront. Caldeirada if the day is cold. Travesseiros from Sintra if Jim swings that way on the Ericeira day. And the local aguardente, once.

Pads

Bring them. The bowls on this coast are concrete, the medical system is fine but you'd rather not test it on holiday.
