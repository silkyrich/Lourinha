Welcome

You've each booked yourselves into a week of skating and not-quite-knowing-anyone in a small town on the Portuguese coast. That's the brief. The town is Lourinhã, an hour north of Lisbon — whitewashed houses, a cliff-backed beach four kilometres west, dinosaur fossils in the cliffs, and a brandy named after the town that you should drink at least once.

The area, properly looked at

Lourinhã sits in the Oeste region — the bit of Portugal between Lisbon and the silver coast. Inside a 90-minute drive you have medieval walled towns, a UNESCO mountain palace complex, the westernmost point of continental Europe, the world's most photographed giant waves, two fishing ports, a brandy region and the Portuguese capital. The eight days are not enough.

Scenic drives

The N247 coast: Peniche to Ericeira

The single best drive in the region. Cliffs, fishing villages, surf beaches, vineyards. Stop at Vimeiro, Areia Branca, Santa Cruz, Praia Azul. About 75 km end to end at the speed it deserves.

2.5h with stops · paved · low traffic outside July/August

Serra de Sintra loop

N375 climbs into the Sintra mountains — hairpins, eucalyptus shade, cool air after the coast. Drop down to Cabo da Roca (westernmost point of continental Europe), then the coast road into Cascais.

3h with stops · paved · busy on weekends

Inland wine country

Lourinhã → Bombarral → Óbidos → Alcobaça. Vineyards, the medieval walled town, and the cistercian monastery of Alcobaça with the tomb of Pedro and Inês — a thousand-year-old love story carved in stone.

Half day · paved · cultural-heavy, light skating

The Nazaré clifftop run

Up the coast via São Martinho do Porto (perfect shell-shaped bay), into Nazaré, up the funicular to Sítio for the giant-wave canyon viewpoint, then a slow road back via Alcobaça.

Full day · paved · dramatic

Mafra & the royal road

The Palácio Nacional de Mafra is the largest baroque palace in Portugal; the surrounding royal hunting forest (Tapada de Mafra) is open to visitors. The N9 between Lourinhã and Mafra is a quiet inland alternative to the motorway.

Half day · paved · low-key

Sintra town

UNESCO site, four fairy-tale palaces in walking distance, vertical streets, tourist-heavy by 10am. Go early or go late. The Pena Palace and Quinta da Regaleira are the famous two; Monserrate is the quietest and arguably the best.

Full day · steep · busy

What's worth a stop on the way to a skate

  • To Peniche — the salt pans south of the causeway; the Berlengas ferry if anyone wants an extra hour at sea.
  • To Caldas — Óbidos (15 min detour), Foz do Arelho (lagoon beach), the Bordallo Pinheiro ceramics factory shop.
  • To Ericeira — Mafra palace en route; or take the cliff road via Santa Cruz for the scenic version.
  • To Lisbon — Belém for the original pastéis, the MAAT for art, the Time Out Market for a chaotic late lunch.

Food, supplies, and where the night goes

Supermarkets in Lourinhã

Three working assumptions, all reliable in this part of Portugal:

  • Pingo Doce — Portuguese chain, the everyday-shop choice. There's at least one in Lourinhã town.
  • Lidl or Intermarché — usually on the edge of town, cheaper for bulk, drinks, snacks.
  • Continente — the largest weekly-shop hypermarket in the region sits in Caldas da Rainha (30 min). Worth a single big trip on day one if the house is self-catering.

Bread and pastries from a town padaria, fish straight off the boat in Peniche or São Martinho do Porto, fruit from any Tuesday/Saturday street market.

Restaurants — what to order, not exactly where

Specific names go out of date fast. Aim for the categories:

  • Marisqueira — shellfish house. Order the arroz de marisco (seafood rice) for the table; percebes (gooseneck barnacles) if you're brave.
  • Casa de pasto — neighbourhood cantine. Daily fixed menu, €10–15, generous, no English. Lunch only, typically.
  • Tasca — a wine-led small joint, often family-run. Petiscos (Portuguese tapas) and house wine.
  • Churrasqueira — chicken on a grill, takeaway-friendly, group-friendly, cheap.

Local specialities worth seeking: caldeirada (fish stew), bacalhau à brás (salt cod, eggs, potato), polvo à lagareiro (whole octopus with olive oil and potatoes), sardinhas assadas (grilled sardines, in season from June).

Add specific names you've eaten well at to the chat — that's how we'll build the gang's restaurant list in real time.

Nightlife

  • Praia da Areia Branca seafront — bars open until late through summer; mellow, mixed-age, drinks-with-a-view.
  • Peniche — surf-bar culture, especially around the old town and the Baleal causeway.
  • Ericeira — the busiest nightlife scene within easy reach; bars, occasional DJs, summer crowd.
  • Caldas da Rainha — student town energy; quieter than the coast but a real bar scene.
  • Lisbon — Bairro Alto and Cais do Sodré — if anyone fancies a proper big-city night; book a designated driver or a hotel.

Portuguese, for skating and for getting out of trouble

Portuguese isn't Spanish. The vowels swallow themselves, the ão sound is a nasal bagpipe, and most locals will be patient with you for trying. A handful of these will buy you a lot of goodwill.

The unavoidables

OláHello
Bom dia / Boa tarde / Boa noiteGood morning / afternoon / evening
Obrigado (men) / Obrigada (women)Thank you — the ending matches who's saying it, not who you're thanking
Por favorPlease
DesculpeSorry / excuse me
Fala inglês?Do you speak English?
A conta, por favorThe bill, please
Duas cervejas, por favorTwo beers, please (use uma for one, três for three, etc.)
Saúde!Cheers

Actually useful for skating

Onde fica a pista de skate?Where's the skatepark?
Podemos andar de skate aqui?Can we skate here?
Sabe um sítio bom para andar?Do you know a good spot to skate?
O chão é liso?Is the surface smooth?
Onde compro um skate / rodas / lixa?Where can I buy a board / wheels / griptape?
Foi brutal!That was sick! (genuine compliment, locals use it too)
Estou bem.I'm fine. (Says the person on the floor.)

Phrases that buy time when something has gone wrong

Desculpe, foi sem querer.Sorry, it wasn't on purpose.
Desculpe a parede, foi o vento.Sorry about the wall, it was the wind.
Pago já.I'll pay right now. (Magic words.)
Já vamos embora, prometo.We're leaving right now, I promise.
Não sou eu o líder, é aquele.I'm not the leader, he is. (Point at the most innocent member of the group.)
Precisamos de gelo. Para o joelho, não para a bebida.We need ice. For the knee, not the drink.
Já morreu alguém neste corrimão?Has anyone died on this rail?
O segurança é rápido?Is the security guard fast?
Somos vinte e um. Faltam dois.There are twenty-one of us. Two are missing.
O meu amigo diz que está bem, mas não está.My friend says he's fine, but he's not.
Mais duas, e uma para o motorista que não bebe.Two more, and one for the driver who isn't drinking.
Para onde vai a noite depois da meia-noite?Where does the night go after midnight?

Pronunciation tip the size of a postcard: Portuguese eats final vowels and slurs unstressed ones. Obrigado sounds closer to brigadu. Está sounds like shtah. Lean into it, don't enunciate, and you'll sound less like a tourist.

Practical bits

Getting there

Fly Lisbon (LIS). A8 motorway north, around an hour. Rent a car between two or three of you — public transport burns the day. The motorway uses an electronic toll transponder; the rental desk will sort it.

Weather

Late May / early June: mid-20s °C, mostly dry, some cloud, reliably windy after lunch. Layer for the evening — the Atlantic cools things off quickly.

Money & plugs

Euros. Cards work nearly everywhere. Keep €30 in cash for small bars and markets. Plugs are Type F (the European two round pins).

Emergencies

112 is the all-services number (police, ambulance, fire). English is understood. The hospital is in Caldas da Rainha; local health centre in Lourinhã.

Pads

Bring them. Concrete bowls and Atlantic salt damp are an unforgiving combination, and the medical system is good but you'd rather not test it.

Group comms

A WhatsApp / Signal group should be circulating before arrival. If you're not in it yet, post your number in the chat below and someone will pull you in.
